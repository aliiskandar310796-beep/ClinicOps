# ClinicOps clinical-operations expansion resilience controls

## Objective

Expand ClinicOps beyond MDR/EUDAMED without losing claim discipline, commercial truth, evidence provenance or operational recoverability.

## Failure modes to prevent

1. **Brand drift** — ClinicOps becomes generic consulting unrelated to clinical operations.
2. **Regulatory overreach** — an evidence signal is presented as a legal/compliance/clinical conclusion.
3. **Speculative product sprawl** — software is built before repeated buyer pain is proven.
4. **Agent sprawl** — temporary execution workers become uncontrolled permanent actors.
5. **Single-market dependency** — one regulation or one buyer type becomes existential to the company.
6. **Single-tool dependency** — delivery cannot proceed if one SaaS/API/connector is unavailable.
7. **Unbounded client work** — adjacent work becomes open-ended expert consulting without scope or activation controls.
8. **False passive-income accounting** — downloads or automation are treated as revenue proof.
9. **Private-data leakage** — prospect/client evidence enters the public repo.
10. **Concurrent-agent overwrite** — parallel workers overwrite newer main or each other's controlled assets.
11. **Concurrent outbound overshoot** — multiple workers independently read stale send counts and exceed company-wide outreach ceilings.

## Portfolio resilience rules

### Name-fit rule

Every adjacent lane must directly support clinical research, medical products, diagnostics/labs, clinical technology or care delivery.

### Evidence-fit rule

ClinicOps must be able to express the workflow with the shared evidence object model:

`source -> scope -> observation -> evidence -> state -> owner -> decision -> closure`

If the main value depends on ClinicOps making a reserved clinical/legal/regulatory decision, the opportunity does not fit the operating model.

### Commercial proof rule

No lane becomes a default offer before:
- 3 qualified conversations;
- 2 independent confirmations of repeated pain;
- 1 E6 or stronger signal.

No recurring subscription is offered as a default until cadence is confirmed by real delivery or buyer evidence.

### Automation rule

Automate only stable repeated steps. Preferred automation targets:
- evidence ingestion;
- normalization/entity resolution;
- deterministic comparison;
- change detection;
- completeness checks;
- queue generation;
- packet assembly;
- reproducible audit trail.

Reserved human decisions remain explicit and fail closed.

## Multi-lane resilience

Maintain at least three commercially testable lanes with shared primitives but different demand drivers:

- TrialOps — sponsor/CRO/study change operations;
- QualityOps — QMS/change/supplier/training operations;
- Clinical AI Ops — AI system/vendor/change governance evidence.

Candidate lanes LabOps, Clinic Vendor/SOP Ops and Controlled Medical Content are held in discovery until they earn promotion.

The point is not diversification for its own sake. The portfolio should reduce dependence on one regulation while maximizing reuse of the same operating engine.

## Delivery fallback

Every sellable ClinicOps review must have a low-infrastructure fallback:
- CSV/JSON/plain-document input accepted where practical;
- browser-local tools remain usable without a ClinicOps backend;
- generated evidence packets are portable;
- deterministic rules are documented;
- human review can continue when an external API is unavailable;
- no client outcome depends solely on one third-party connector.

## Agent / fleet containment

Temporary workers inherit the parent agent's scope and cannot expand it.

Workers may:
- research public sources;
- compare market/problem hypotheses;
- draft bounded offer/test assets;
- produce code/tests on isolated branches;
- run deterministic validation;
- surface commercial signals.

Workers may not independently:
- publish unsupported regulatory claims;
- create client commitments beyond authorized scope;
- override qualified human review;
- put E4+ buyer evidence in the public repo;
- weaken integrity/CI gates;
- treat internal scores as external validation;
- execute cold outbound outside the single-writer outbound control.

## Outbound single-writer rule

Cold outbound is a shared scarce resource, not an agent-local action.

Before any cold send:
1. one designated send controller reads the live shared Sent mailbox for the current company day;
2. it reconciles the count with any other known ClinicOps mailbox used that day;
3. it applies permanent suppression, Denmark cold-email prohibition and domain/thread dedupe;
4. if the cold-send count is unknown, stale, at or above the company ceiling, or concurrency cannot be reconciled, the send fails closed;
5. workers may prepare candidates and copy, but only the send controller performs the external send.

This rule exists because independent workers can each be locally correct while collectively exceeding a shared limit.

## Kill-switch conditions

Pause a lane immediately when:
- a critical integrity or privacy issue appears;
- delivery requires an expertise/authorization level ClinicOps cannot responsibly provide;
- repeated buyer conversations contradict the core pain thesis;
- customer acquisition depends on claims ClinicOps cannot evidence;
- the lane needs a bespoke architecture with little reusable ClinicOps value;
- ten qualified buyer conversations yield no meaningful E6 signal;
- shared outbound or another company-level control is breached and the root cause is not yet contained.

## Expansion conditions

Promote a lane when:
- E6/E7 evidence exists;
- at least two independent customers expose substantially the same repeated evidence structure;
- the delivery can be bounded and reviewed;
- recurring cadence is real rather than assumed;
- unit economics improve with reuse;
- automation can reduce reconciliation work without taking human authority.

## Company-level resilience metric

Track four distinct proofs, never collapse them into one score:

1. **commercial proof** — buyer action, activation, payment, repeat;
2. **delivery proof** — accepted outputs and controlled closure;
3. **technical proof** — deterministic tests, CI, recoverability;
4. **governance proof** — claim boundaries, human review, private-data containment.

A lane can be technically excellent and commercially unvalidated. A sale can occur before the delivery system is resilient. Both distinctions must remain visible.
