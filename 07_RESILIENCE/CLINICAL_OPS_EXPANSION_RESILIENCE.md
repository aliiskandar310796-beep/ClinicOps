# ClinicOps seven-segment resilience controls

Status: ACTIVE CONTROL LAYER.

## Objective

Run ClinicOps as a live multi-segment clinical-operations company without losing claim discipline, commercial truth, evidence provenance, privacy containment, delivery portability or recoverability under agent concurrency.

## Failure modes this layer is designed to contain

1. Brand drift into generic consulting unrelated to clinical operations.
2. Regulatory or clinical overreach where a machine signal becomes a reserved human conclusion.
3. Speculative product sprawl before repeated buyer pain is proven.
4. Agent sprawl and concurrent writes that overwrite newer controlled state.
5. Shared outbound overshoot caused by independent locally-correct workers.
6. Single-regulation, single-buyer, single-geography or single-channel dependency.
7. Single-tool dependency that blocks delivery when a connector/API fails.
8. Unbounded services that become open-ended expert consulting.
9. False recurring/passive revenue assumptions without real cadence.
10. Private buyer/client evidence leaking into public repository artifacts.
11. Portfolio starvation where one fashionable lane consumes all execution capacity.

## Shared evidence primitive

Every segment must be expressible through the same operating spine:

`approved source -> declared scope -> observation -> evidence reference -> deterministic state -> unresolved question -> accountable owner -> human decision -> closure evidence -> change history`

If the main value requires ClinicOps itself to make a reserved clinical, legal, regulatory, quality, safety or release decision, the work does not fit the model.

## Portfolio containment

The live portfolio currently uses seven segments with an 8% protected floor and 20% hard ceiling. Reallocation is evidence-weighted and bounded to five percentage points per weekly cycle unless a documented kill switch applies.

No segment may be silently set to zero simply because another segment is temporarily fashionable. No segment may exceed the ceiling without explicit evidence and a documented strategic decision.

## Commercial truth model

Operational availability and commercial proof are separate fields.

- A lane may be LIVE_READY because ClinicOps has a bounded deliverable and qualified human boundary.
- A lane may still have only E1 public evidence.
- Commercial validation is earned through external buyer action, not internal readiness.
- E4+ buyer evidence remains private.

No recurring subscription becomes the default offer until cadence is confirmed by real buyer or delivery evidence. No lower-touch product is promoted merely because it can be built.

## Automation boundary

Automate stable repeated steps such as:

- evidence ingestion and normalization;
- entity resolution;
- deterministic comparison;
- change detection;
- completeness checks;
- queue generation;
- evidence packet assembly;
- reproducible audit history;
- distribution preparation and internal routing.

Do not automate reserved decision authority. Uncertainty, missing evidence and conflicting sources remain visible and route to a named human owner.

## Multi-tool delivery fallback

Every sellable review must have a low-infrastructure fallback where practical:

- CSV/JSON/plain-document inputs;
- browser-local tooling;
- portable evidence packets;
- documented deterministic rules;
- human review independent of one external API;
- no customer outcome dependent solely on one connector, model provider or SaaS platform.

## Fleet containment

Workers may research public sources, draft assets, prepare candidates, build code/tests on isolated branches, run deterministic validation and surface commercial signals.

Workers may not independently:

- weaken CI, Integrity Gate or claim controls;
- publish unsupported regulatory/compliance claims;
- create commitments outside approved service scope;
- override qualified human review;
- put E4+ buyer evidence in the public repo;
- count internal activity as external validation;
- execute cold outbound without company-wide admission control.

## Shared outbound single-writer control

Cold outbound is a company-level scarce resource, not an agent-local action.

Before any cold send, the controller must reconcile a fresh company-wide snapshot, daily cold count, pending reservations, permanent suppressions, destination jurisdiction, domain/thread history and branded sender state.

If the snapshot is incomplete, stale, destination jurisdiction is unknown, the domain already has prior contact, Denmark is the cold destination, a permanent suppression applies, or the shared daily capacity is exhausted, the send fails closed. Non-cold distribution may continue.

## Kill switches

Pause or sharply reduce a lane when:

- privacy or integrity risk cannot be bounded;
- delivery requires expertise/authorization ClinicOps cannot responsibly provide;
- ten qualified buyer conversations yield no meaningful commercial progression;
- acquisition depends on unsupported claims;
- delivery becomes highly bespoke with little reusable ClinicOps structure;
- repeated buyer evidence contradicts the pain thesis;
- shared outbound or another company-level control is breached and root cause is not contained;
- an external dependency becomes a single point of failure without fallback.

## Promotion conditions

Increase capacity when external evidence shows:

- E6/E7 progression;
- at least two independent customers exposing substantially the same repeated evidence structure;
- bounded delivery with accepted human-review boundaries;
- real recurring cadence rather than assumed cadence;
- improving unit economics through reuse;
- automation can remove reconciliation work without assuming authority.

## Health model

Never collapse the company into one score. Track separately:

1. Commercial proof — buyer action, activation, payment, repeat/referral.
2. Delivery proof — accepted outputs, closure quality, turnaround, rework.
3. Distribution proof — qualified reach by segment/channel and inbound conversion.
4. Recurring proof — cadence, renewal and retained evidence workflows.
5. Technical proof — deterministic tests, CI, portability and recoverability.
6. Governance proof — claim boundaries, human review, privacy and suppression compliance.

A lane can be technically excellent and commercially weak. A sale can exist before delivery is resilient. Both states must remain visible.
