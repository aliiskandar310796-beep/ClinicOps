## Intro

Class III and implantable devices carry the heaviest MDR transition workload, and the pressure rarely comes from a single record. It comes from reconciling legacy and MDR registration paths, certificate timing, and SS(C)P duty across a whole portfolio at once.

ClinicOps helps manufacturers and authorised representatives map that portfolio, separate what is on a legacy path from what is on an MDR path, surface the timing questions, and decide what needs human review first.

## Problem

### The workload is not one certificate. It is a portfolio moving at different speeds.

For a class III or implantable portfolio, teams often hold public EUDAMED records, notified-body certificate dates, internal transition evidence and SS(C)P material that were never designed to be read together.

The hard part is not adding another data point. It is knowing which devices are approaching a timing constraint, which identifiers still need reconciling, and which SS(C)P obligations sit with the manufacturer as duty holder.

ClinicOps turns that fragmented evidence into a prioritised queue while keeping machine-detected signals separate from human regulatory judgement.

## SS(C)P duty boundary

Under MDR Article 32, the manufacturer is the duty holder for drawing up the summary of safety and clinical performance (SS(C)P) for applicable implantable and class III devices, subject to the Article 32 exclusions. [CO-CLM-0001]

ClinicOps does not assume that duty and does not replace the manufacturer's RA/QA responsibility. The service organises the evidence and surfaces the questions around that duty; the regulatory decision stays with the responsible party.

## Legacy identifier screening

For EUDAMED legacy-device identification, the EUDAMED DI uses the B- prefix and stands in place of a Basic UDI-DI for the legacy registration model. Identifier structure alone does not establish the complete regulatory or SS(C)P state of a device. [CO-CLM-0004]

ClinicOps therefore uses identifier structure as a screening input, not as a standalone compliance conclusion.
