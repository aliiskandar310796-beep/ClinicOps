## Intro

Regulatory intelligence, for a MedTech operation, is not a feed of alerts and it is not a compliance verdict. It is a disciplined way of turning scattered public and internal signals into evidence a regulatory team can act on, with the actor-role and duty-holder context kept intact.

ClinicOps applies machine breadth to find and classify the signal, and human judgement to decide what it means. RA/QA and the manufacturer keep every regulatory decision.

## Problem

### More signal is not the problem. Turning signal into reviewable evidence is.

Portfolio teams can already see EUDAMED records, certificate timing, Basic UDI-DI information, SS(C)P material and market-language documents. What they lack is a repeatable way to reconcile those inputs, hold on to who is responsible for each, and decide what deserves human review first.

Without that discipline, screening either overreaches into unsupported allegations or drowns the real questions in noise.

## What ClinicOps does

### Machine breadth. Human judgement. Evidence that survives review.

ClinicOps is built around one operating model:

1. **capture the signal** — pull the public and supplied inputs across a portfolio;
2. **classify the evidence** — sort what is a screening signal from what is a conclusion;
3. **preserve actor-role and duty-holder context** — keep manufacturer, authorised representative, importer and notified-body roles attached to the evidence;
4. **block unsupported claims** — never convert a missing field or a structural signal into a compliance allegation;
5. **prioritise the work** — order the portfolio by what needs human review first;
6. **deliver a human-reviewed action plan** — hand RA/QA a queue, not a verdict.

Under MDR Article 32, the manufacturer is the duty holder for drawing up the SS(C)P for applicable implantable and class III devices, subject to the Article 32 exclusions. ClinicOps preserves that duty-holder context instead of flattening the workflow into generic document checking. [CO-CLM-0001]

## Evidence-input boundary

Public-register screening is an evidence input, not a compliance determination or a full-register audit. A B-prefixed EUDAMED DI signal, for example, is useful for structural screening but does not by itself establish a device's full regulatory or SS(C)P state. [CO-CLM-0004]

ClinicOps regulatory intelligence is designed for:

## What ClinicOps does not promise

- no end-to-end public-register audit claim when API reachability is incomplete;
- no compliance allegation from a missing public link or field alone;
- no automatic conversion of an observed system pattern or structural signal into law;
- no invented statistics or census counts in place of a verifiable source;
- no replacement for notified-body, legal or manufacturer-controlled evidence, or for RA/QA sign-off, where those inputs are required.
