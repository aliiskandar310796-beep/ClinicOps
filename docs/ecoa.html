COA and eCOA, from instrument to first participant

An electronic outcome assessment is a small piece of a study that has to be right in every language and on every device before the first participant starts. This section maps the work, explains the four assessment types, covers what changes in Denmark and the Nordics, and offers four free tools that run in your browser. Nothing is sent to ClinicOps.

Checklist: overseeing a provider of study technology or services

A template for the sponsor or CRO team that has handed work to a provider such as an eCOA vendor. It is a planning aid, not a compliance determination, and it does not quote any standard; check the requirements that apply to your study.

  1. Duties. Write down who does what, including what stays with the sponsor, in a simple responsibility table.
  2. Written agreement before work. Scope, data handling, change control, access, exit and file hand-over are agreed in writing first.
  3. Provider assessment. Record how you checked the provider is fit for this task, and when you will check again.
  4. Evidence log. Keep what the provider showed you, with dates, in one place a monitor or auditor can open.
  5. Measures and escalation. Agree a few measures, who reviews them and how often, and who is told when one goes wrong.
  6. Exit. Know in advance what you receive if the provider stops, and how a successor picks up.

How the pieces work together

Start with the type finder to list what the study collects. The burden estimator shows whether the schedule asks too much of a participant, and the planner shows whether the languages fit the timeline. The workbench then carries the plan through the build, the testing and the changes.

What these are not. They organise what you enter and show what follows from it. They do not choose instruments, confirm licences, say what burden is acceptable or give regulatory advice. Where a figure is a published planning range, the page shows its source and lets you replace it.
