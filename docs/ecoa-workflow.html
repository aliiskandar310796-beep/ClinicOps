eCOA workflow map, from kick-off to close-out

Ten stages that an electronic outcome-assessment study passes through, what has to be settled at each, what it leaves behind, and which tool on this site helps. It is provider-neutral: it describes the work, not any one vendor's platform.

Reference · sources checked 2026-11-14

Boundary. A map of the work built from published guidance and consensus papers. It makes no claim about how any particular eCOA platform is configured, and the order shown is the usual one, not a rule: licensing and translation start early and overlap the build. The ISPOR validation task force frames validation as a life cycle that runs from requirements to decommissioning, which is why the map ends where it does.

Running through every stage

  • Change management. A wording or schedule change after sign-off can need the instrument developer's or copyright holder's agreement, and for some changes new evidence. The 2025 translation paper says changes during implementation should be discussed with developers and copyright holders. Record every change, who approved it and what it touched.
  • Risk. Keep one register. The usual sources are a licence not yet granted, a language version still in review, a late specification change, devices or own-device access, and a testing window squeezed by earlier delays. Every risk gets an owner and a date.
  • Education. A sponsor choosing electronic collection for the first time needs the same explanation of equivalence, validation and licensing that an experienced team already has. Say what the study will and will not be able to show.
  • Communication. Sponsor, provider, translation, copyright holders, sites and, where relevant, patient representatives each need to know what is decided and what is waiting on them. The workbench keeps decisions in one place so that a question has a single answer.

What good looks like at the end

Anyone running the study can say, for each assessment: who answers, on what, in which language, when, what happens if they do not, and who may change it. The post-project review adds what the plan got wrong, which is more useful to the next study than any template.
