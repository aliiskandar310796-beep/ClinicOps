eCOA delivery workbench

Electronic outcome assessments reach participants late for ordinary reasons: a licence that was not asked for, a language version still in review, a testing window squeezed by everything before it. This workbench keeps those dependencies in one place and runs them as a staged project: a plan per instrument and language, review and testing checklists, a risk register, change control and a report for the people who decide. It runs in your browser; nothing is sent to ClinicOps.

Free tool · Sanitized prototype

Boundary. A sanitized prototype of method, built from published guidance and consensus papers listed in the source register (each checked on 2026-11-14). It structures what you record and shows what follows from it. It is not a compliance determination, not a validated system and not a system of record. Acceptance, go-live, data changes and every regulatory or clinical judgement stay with the sponsor, the investigator and their qualified staff. Do not enter participant data.

Nothing is stored between visits. Export the workspace file to keep your work.

Business case

Why collect these assessments electronically in this study, and is that still true? In one diary study of adults with chronic pain, paper-diary compliance was reported as 90% but measured at 11% (30-minute window); with an electronic diary the measured figure was 94% (Stone 2002). That is the kind of benefit a business case should state in numbers and re-confirm at each gate.

Tolerances

The seven performance targets of PRINCE2. Inside a tolerance the project manager acts; beyond it the project board decides. Measures against these limits are under Progress.

An eCOA build has more parties than most study systems: sponsor functions, a provider, a translation vendor, copyright holders, sites. Give each a project role, so that every sign-off has a name behind it.

Plans

The plan is a list of products, one per instrument and language. Each has its own path: licence, translation, screenshot review, copyright-holder sign-off, submission. The status column reports what the recorded states imply; it does not judge whether a step was done well.

Protocol schedule against the design specification

Paste the schedule of assessments from the protocol and the schedule built into the specification, one line per assessment: instrument; timepoint; window (window optional). The protocol is the reference. The comparison reports differences and does not decide which side is right.

Risk

Exposure is read from a visible matrix, not calculated behind the page: high when probability and impact are both high or one is high and the other medium; medium when both are medium or one is high and the other low; otherwise low. The seeded risks come with their basis and a suggested response. None is pre-rated: probability and impact belong to your study.

Issues

One register for problems, requests for change, off-specifications, assumptions and decisions. Together with the risk register it exports as a single log from the Progress tab.

Change control: which surfaces should a change reach?

Tick what the change is. The rule table lists every surface it is expected to reach, with the reason. Each surface stays open until it has an owner and closure evidence, or a recorded reason it does not apply. The rules are a ClinicOps method; add surfaces your study has that the table does not.

Type of change

Data change requests

A correction to participant-entered data is requested, justified and authorised on the site side, and leaves a trace. A request closed without a recorded investigator authorisation is flagged for qualified review.

Progress

Management by exception: each measure has a target and a tolerance limit. Between the two, the tolerance is threatened; beyond the limit it is an exception for the project board. Targets and limits are blank until you set them.

Completion monitor

Counts only, no participant data: entries expected and completed per group. Paste group, expected, completed, one group per line, or add rows by hand.

Tool assessment (documented-tool record)

Purpose, requirements, test evidence, limitations, change log

Purpose. Structure the delivery of electronic outcome assessments in a clinical study as a staged project, and show what the recorded information implies: which products are waiting on which step, which change surfaces lack closure evidence, which tolerances are exceeded. A structuring aid; never the system of record.

Requirements. R1 Every checklist item and seeded risk names its basis; every cited source is in the source register. R2 Status values are derived by fixed rules from recorded states and can be read from the rule description on the page. R3 A step or checklist item marked not applicable counts as open until a reason is recorded. R4 A change surface closes only with an owner and closure evidence. R5 No targets, tolerance limits or risk ratings are pre-filled outside the fictional example. R6 All processing is local: no network call after page load, no browser storage; the workspace file is the only persistence. R7 Imported files are validated and rendered as text only. R8 Exports neutralise spreadsheet formulas.

Test evidence (v1.0.0, 2026-11-14). Automated rule tests cover product status, schedule comparison, exposure bands, change-surface mapping and closure, script generation, tolerance status, completion counts, export safety and workspace validation. A browser journey loads the fictional example, exercises each tab, confirms no request leaves the page and checks layout from 320 px. Both run in the repository's continuous integration on every change.

Known limitations. Not validated for use as a regulated system. The change-surface rule table and several checklist items are ClinicOps method and may not fit a given study or provider. Sequencing of screenshot review and acceptance testing differs between providers; the plan does not enforce one order. Source documents may change after the review date. No real study has been delivered with this workbench.

Change log. v1.0.0 (2026-11-14): first release; nine checklists, 19 seeded risks, 10 change types across 14 surfaces, 15 testing areas.

When the open items are Danish-language work

Screens that read like a translation, system text nobody reviewed, participant material that does not match the build: if the open items on your plan are the Danish version itself, that is work ClinicOps takes on, reviewed by a native Danish speaker with an MSc in Medicine (Translational Medicine). The Swedish and Norwegian checks on this page are review prompts for your own reviewers. ClinicOps does not hold the provider, sponsor or investigator role in a study.
