Choosing an EU authorised representative.

ClinicOps is not an authorised representative and is not offering authorised-representative mandates. It has no person responsible for regulatory compliance (PRRC) available to it, which an authorised representative needs. This page lists what to check when you choose one.

What to check before you sign a mandate

  1. Written mandate. Under MDR Article 11 the representative acts under a mandate that sets out the tasks it performs for the manufacturer and that it accepts in writing.
  2. A PRRC at its disposal. MDR and IVDR Article 15(6) require an authorised representative to have at least one person responsible for regulatory compliance permanently and continuously at its disposal. Ask who that person is and how their qualifications are shown. The Commission guidance is MDCG 2019-7.
  3. Liability and cover. Where a manufacturer outside the Union has not met its Article 10 obligations, the representative is legally liable for defective devices on the same basis as, and jointly and severally with, the manufacturer (Article 11(5)). Ask what liability or insurance cover applies and what it excludes.
  4. EUDAMED. Look the representative up in the public EUDAMED actor register and agree how the mandate and your device registrations are recorded.
  5. Continuity. Ask who acts if the named person is absent, and how authority requests and vigilance reports reach you.
  6. Your own duties. A representative does not take over the manufacturer's general obligations. Keep your own records and decisions in order.

What ClinicOps does instead

ClinicOps reconciles approved device information against Basic UDI-DI / UDI-DI records, certificates, declarations, IFUs, labels, SS(C)P and controlled documents. The output is a reviewer-ready exception queue, not an automatic compliance verdict. It can support a manufacturer or an authorised representative; it does not replace one.
