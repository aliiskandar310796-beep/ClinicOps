Working with partners: narrow units, one accountable owner.

ClinicOps does the work itself. It takes bounded units of work from language-service providers, CROs and eCOA vendors, and manufacturers and authorised representatives, as your subcontractor and in your format. An outside specialist is approached only for a defined question, with your approval beforehand. There is no standing team.

Rules as of 16 November 2026 · last verified against the sources named below · next review by 14 May 2027.

Who this is for

Language-service providers

You hold the translation, the glossary and the translation memory. ClinicOps checks around the translation step, not the translation itself. See the section for language-service providers.

CROs and eCOA vendors

Country-layer fact checks for Denmark: a read of your start-up list against the public Danish authority pages, citing the page each point rests on. Not a legal opinion, and nothing is submitted on your behalf.

Manufacturers and authorised representatives

Identifier and certificate reconciliation, pre-submission completeness reads and SS(C)P and translation inventories. ClinicOps does not take an authorised-representative or PRRC mandate; see what to check when you choose an authorised representative.

Units a partner can hand over

Identifier and certificate reconciliation

Compare a list of devices, UDI-DIs and certificates against the public EUDAMED records and your own master data. You get an exception queue and a closure packet. Records that differ are listed as differences to resolve, not as findings about compliance.

Pre-submission completeness read

A read of a submission pack or a change package against a checklist you supply or approve: what is missing, inconsistent between documents, or not yet decided. It does not assess whether the content meets a requirement.

Native Danish review

Danish-language review of clinical and device text against the approved source, with open questions routed to the accountable owner. Where you supply a glossary or translation memory, it prevails over ClinicOps wording. For medicines safety communications, see the DHPC checker.

Handover note and work product

These are two different documents, and only one of them carries ClinicOps’ name.

Signed handover note

For every unit ClinicOps signs a handover note stating what was done, by whom and what was not. It stays between you and ClinicOps.

White-label work product

Where you agree white-label work in writing, the exception queue, packet or review text is delivered in your format for you to put your name on, and carries no ClinicOps signature. Without that agreement it is delivered under the ClinicOps name.

Rules that do not bend

  • No silent subcontracting. If anyone other than ClinicOps will touch your material, you are told who does what before work starts, and you can say no.
  • NDA first. Confidential material is exchanged only after a signed confidentiality agreement. Do not send patient or personal data.
  • One accountable owner. ClinicOps remains your counterparty and is accountable for what it delivers, even when a specialist contributed. Specialists are not represented as ClinicOps employees, officers, notified bodies, competent authorities or decision owners.
  • Your name stays yours. ClinicOps does not contact your clients and does not name you or them anywhere without written permission.
  • Inbound only. ClinicOps does not cold-contact organisations to sell partner work.

Data, liability and price

Data default

Regulated product data only: EUDAMED and UDI records, certificate details, label and IFU text, declarations. No personal data. If a unit cannot be done without personal data, a written data-processing agreement is needed first, or the unit is declined.

Liability

Working without a written limit of liability is not offered. The limit is agreed in writing for each unit of work, before records are exchanged.

Price

No price is shown on this page. A price is quoted after a written scope.

For language-service providers

ClinicOps is a partner to the translation step, not a competitor to it. It works upstream and downstream of the translation and leaves the translation, and its quality scoring, with you.

What ClinicOps does

Checks whether the approved source text is complete and consistent before language versions multiply; checks whether label, IFU, SS(C)P and registry text still agree with the approved source after a change; maps which language versions a change touches; and, where agreed, reads Danish text within your glossary and translation memory.

What ClinicOps does not do

No translation. No LQA scoring of anyone’s translation. No certification of translations. No compliance sign-off. No authorised-representative or PRRC role. Where a rule on language differs by country, ClinicOps links to the authority’s own page and does not restate it as its own.

Not in scope

  • Translation, and LQA scoring of anyone’s translation.
  • Certification of any kind: of translations, devices, systems or people.
  • Any mandate or appointed role, including authorised representative and PRRC.
  • Bulk upload or registration of data in EUDAMED on a client’s behalf.
  • Acting as a legal representative, including a sponsor’s legal representative for a clinical trial.
  • Opinion on whether any device, trial or document meets a requirement, and any statement about a named manufacturer or party.
  • Contact with notified bodies or authorities, and personal data by default.

A CV, job title or language skill is not treated as proof of fit. The assignment has to match the person’s verified work type, jurisdiction, language and professional boundary. Availability, confidentiality, commercial terms and any professional-authorisation requirements are checked before activation. Contact details, rates, credential documents and contracts are kept in an access-controlled system, and a named public profile appears only with consent and verified wording.

Notified-body conflict check

Annex VII point 1.2 of Regulation (EU) 2017/745 sets independence and impartiality rules for notified bodies. Point 1.2.4 bars personnel who formerly worked for, or gave device consultancy to, a specific client from being assigned to assess that client for three years. ClinicOps uses the same three-year look-back as its own check: before work starts, ClinicOps and any specialist declare any employment by, or contract with, a notified body in the past three years, and any role that could make the output a review of their own earlier work. Where that conflicts with the end client, the assignment is declined or reassigned. The Regulation sets these duties for notified bodies, not for partners.

Professional boundary. Legal, regulatory, clinical, safety, quality and release decisions remain with the explicitly accountable person or organisation. A specialist’s observation becomes evidence in the review; it does not automatically become your decision. There is no standing network: no specialty or country is permanently staffed, and no list of specialists is published.

Sample scope (fictional)

Example only, with invented data. A real scope is agreed in writing before work starts.

Unit

Identifier reconciliation for a fictional portfolio of 120 device records.

You provide

A CSV with device name, UDI-DI, Basic UDI-DI, certificate number and manufacturer reference; the date you want the work finished; the person who will decide each exception.

You receive

An exception queue (each row: the two records that differ, the field, the source of each), a closure packet showing what was compared and what was not, and a short list of open questions for your decision owner.

Accepted when

Every input row appears in the output once; every exception names both sources; nothing is labelled compliant or non-compliant; questions are phrased so that they can be answered yes, no or “not known”.

Stops at

Delivery of the packet. Changes to the scope go in writing; corrections to your own records are yours to make. Files are handed over on exit.

Not included

Opinion on whether any device meets a requirement, contact with notified bodies or authorities, authorised-representative duties, and anything outside the CSV.
