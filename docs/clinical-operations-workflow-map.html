Clinical operations workflow map

Start-up work in a clinical study does not run one step after another. Authorisation, contracts, systems, supply and site readiness run side by side, and several of them have to be ready before the first subject can start. This page maps that work from planning to archive: what has to be settled, what each step leaves behind, who usually owns it, and, only where the legal text was read, the clock that applies.

Rules as of 16 November 2026 · last verified against the sources named below · next review by 14 December 2026.

Reference · version 1 · sources opened 16 November 2026

Boundary. A planning aid, not advice. It is a map of the work, built from the Regulation text and published guidance. It is not a regulatory, legal or quality determination, not a substitute for the sponsor's quality assurance, regulatory affairs or counsel, and not a statement about any named study, sponsor or device. Protocols, Member States and ethics bodies can set shorter periods or further steps than any figure shown here.

Clock Initial application Drug trials (CTR)

Validation 10 days; Part I 45 days; Part II 45 days; decision within 5 days of the later of the two

Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 Articles 5 to 8 · CO-CLM-0021 · Reading date: see the registered entry · Review due 2027-02-14

Registered wording and limits (CO-CLM-0021)

Under Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 Articles 5 to 8, for an initial clinical trial application: validation takes 10 days (up to 25 with a request for information); the Part I assessment takes 45 days from the validation date in steps of 26, 12 and 7 days, extended by up to 31 days for a request for information and by up to 50 days for expert consultation on advanced-therapy and Annex point 1 products; Part II takes 45 days, extended by up to 31 days for a request for information; the decision follows within 5 days of the later of the Part I reporting date and the end of Part II. The EMA CTIS Sponsor Handbook states 60 days without and 106 days with requests for information, and describes a pause of all timers from 22 December to 8 January.

Limits. Read from the Official Journal text (Publications Office) as consolidated to 5 December 2022; later amendments were not checked. The winter pause is an EMA/CTCG practice described in the Sponsor Handbook, not in the Regulation, and the 2026-27 dates rest on its generic wording. Weekend and holiday roll-forward follows the handbook and Regulation 1182/71 Article 3; holiday calendars belong to the Member State and are not built in. Where a request for information falls inside an assessment is not fixed by the Regulation, so the tool places it at the end of the assessment it extends. The 156-day advanced-therapy maximum is ClinicOps arithmetic, not a stated figure. Planning aid, not legal advice.

Clock Application and authorisation Device investigations (MDR)

Validation notice within 10 days; sponsor response up to 10 days, extendable by 20; authorisation within 45 days of validation, extendable by 20

MDR Article 70 · CO-CLM-0133 · Read on 2026-11-16 · Review due 2027-02-14

Not shown National additions, ethics committee steps and fees are not shown. Read the Member State authority's current page. Device investigations (MDR)

Registered wording and limits (CO-CLM-0133)

Regulation (EU) 2017/745 Article 70: within 10 days of receiving an application for a clinical investigation the Member State notifies the sponsor whether the investigation falls within the scope of the Regulation and whether the application is complete (70(1)). Where it finds the investigation out of scope or the application incomplete, it sets a time limit of maximum 10 days for the sponsor to comment or complete the application, which it may extend by a maximum of 20 days, and it notifies the sponsor within 5 days of receipt of the comments or information (70(3)). It may extend each of the periods in paragraphs 1 and 3 by a further 5 days (70(4)). The sponsor updates the documentation and notifies the Member State within one week of any change (70(2)). For investigational class I devices and non-invasive class IIa and IIb devices the investigation may start after the validation date, unless national law provides otherwise and subject to there being no negative opinion of an ethics committee (70(7)(a)). For other investigational devices the Member State notifies its authorisation within 45 days of the validation date, and may extend that period by a further 20 days to consult experts (70(7)(b)).

Registered wording and limits (CO-CLM-0135)

Regulation (EU) 2017/745 Article 75: where the sponsor intends to introduce modifications to the clinical investigation likely to have a substantial impact on the safety, health or rights of the subjects or on the robustness or reliability of the data, it notifies the Member State(s) concerned within one week of the reasons for and the nature of the modifications (75(1)). Registered wording and limits (CO-CLM-0131)

Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 Article 58(1): unless other Union law requires archiving for a longer period, the sponsor and the investigator archive the content of the clinical trial master file for at least 25 years after the end of the clinical trial. The medical files of subjects are archived in accordance with national law.

Limits. Read on 16 November 2026 in the consolidated text of 5 December 2022 on EUR-Lex; the Official Journal text as originally published was read as well and the figures agreed. Other Union law, national law and contracts may require longer; the master-file content and the archiving arrangements are in Articles 57 and 58 and are not summarised here.
