# Integrity Gate — commercial and economic validation protocol

Status: **ACTIVE TEST / SOFTWARE-VALIDATED / ECONOMICALLY UNVALIDATED**

This protocol exists to prevent a technically strong Integrity Gate from being mistaken for a business. The next proof required is willingness to pay, measurable review economics, and a repeatable buying path.

## Commercial job to be done

Test whether manufacturers, authorised representatives and regulatory teams will pay ClinicOps to reduce the effort and uncertainty involved in proving that a regulated fact still agrees across the controlled sources and downstream surfaces they declare relevant.

The customer is not buying an AI compliance opinion. The customer is buying a bounded, human-reviewed evidence reconciliation and change-propagation work product.

## Target buyers

Prioritise buyers with all of the following:

1. multiple controlled regulatory surfaces or systems that should agree;
2. repeated changes, releases, submissions or portfolio handoffs;
3. named RA/QA ownership;
4. a visible cost of manual cross-checking, ambiguity, rework or delay;
5. authority to request a quote, sponsor a pilot, involve procurement or identify a budget owner.

Best initial roles:

- Regulatory Operations / Regulatory Affairs leaders;
- UDI / EUDAMED owners;
- RA/QA managers responsible for controlled document or product-data change;
- authorised-representative portfolio leads;
- specialist regulatory consultancies with repeated client portfolios.

## External economic anchors — dated 2026-09-14

These are market-calibration signals, not ClinicOps price claims and not evidence of willingness to pay for Integrity Gate.

- EUDAMate publicly lists EUDAMED data-preparation pricing from €39 for 1–2 devices, then volume pricing per device, plus optional Registry Care subscriptions from €29/month to €349/month.
- EudaMed.com publicly lists UDI submission packages from €600/year for up to 50 UDI-DI submissions through €3,950/year for up to 1,000, plus separately priced support/consultancy.
- qmsWrapper is publicly listed from €3,499/year for its Foundation QMS tier.
- EudaMed Check publicly lists annual monitoring / intelligence tiers from hundreds to several thousand euros depending on scope and volume.

Interpretation: ClinicOps should not compete as the cheapest submission or CSV-preparation utility. The wedge should be a high-trust reconciliation / change-control service where human review, source authority and cross-surface evidence matter.

## Price experiments — internal only

Do not publish these as standard pricing until the price gate is explicitly approved. Use them as willingness-to-pay probes in qualified conversations.

### P0 — diagnostic floor

**€390** fixed-scope diagnostic.

Use only when the buyer is interested but cannot yet sponsor the primary pilot. Keep scope smaller than the primary pilot and do not turn it into open-ended unpaid consulting.

### P1 — primary Integrity Gate pilot hypothesis

**€750 fixed fee**.

Target scope:

- one device family / Basic UDI-DI group;
- 1–3 controlled sources;
- 2–6 downstream surfaces;
- 3–10 fields;
- optional one approved change event;
- one review cycle;
- human-reviewed evidence bundle.

Commercial question:

> If we can run this bounded scope against your real workflow, preserve the source evidence, and return a human-reviewed discrepancy pack with reviewer effort and false positives measured, is €750 a plausible pilot purchase?

Record the answer as one of: `YES`, `YES_IF`, `NO_PRICE`, `NO_PROBLEM`, `NO_BUDGET`, `PROCUREMENT_REQUIRED`.

### P2 — expanded pilot probe

**€1,250 fixed fee**.

Probe only when the buyer has more surfaces / fields, a material deadline or explicit budget ownership. Do not discount down from this number without recording why.

### Recurring hypothesis

Do not sell a recurring subscription merely because the software can monitor. A recurring offer earns the right to exist only when at least two independent paid-pilot buyers say the same change/reconciliation problem recurs and want it checked repeatedly.

## Economic discovery questions

Ask for numbers, not adjectives:

1. How many change / release events trigger cross-surface review in a typical month?
2. How many systems/documents/surfaces are usually checked per event?
3. Roughly how many reviewer minutes are spent per surface or per event today?
4. Which role performs the review, and what loaded internal hourly cost range is reasonable for business-case purposes?
5. How often does the team need a second pass because source authority or downstream status is unclear?
6. What happens when a mismatch is found late: extra review, delayed submission, document rework, escalation, or something else?
7. Who owns budget for a bounded diagnostic or pilot?
8. What price would be trivial, plausible, difficult, and impossible for this scope?
9. If the pilot works, how often would the workflow need to run?

Never claim avoided regulatory loss, compliance, or submission acceptance from these answers.

## Pilot measurement contract

Every live paid pilot should collect, privately:

- input preparation minutes;
- automated rule checks;
- change-propagation checks;
- number of findings by machine state;
- reviewer minutes;
- reviewer disposition for every finding: `CONFIRMED`, `RESOLVED_BY_CONTEXT`, `NOT_ACTIONABLE`, `FALSE_POSITIVE`;
- false-positive rate;
- client-reported current-state manual review time for comparable scope;
- total ClinicOps delivery time;
- buyer decision after delivery: stop / repeat / expand / recurring / referral;
- invoice and payment evidence when applicable.

Do not claim labour saving until comparable before/after evidence exists.

## Commercial evidence ladder for Integrity Gate

- `E0`: build, docs, synthetic fixture — not demand.
- `E1`: external regulatory/market signal — not buyer evidence.
- `E2`: qualified target account — not buyer evidence.
- `E3`: targeted demand test delivered — execution only.
- `E4`: real buyer reply / qualified conversation.
- `E5`: buyer confirms repeated reconciliation/change-propagation pain and consequence.
- `E6`: priced-scope request, accepted pilot proposal, procurement step or identifiable budget owner.
- `E7`: paid/activated pilot.
- `E8`: accepted delivery and/or payment.
- `E9`: repeat, expansion, recurring engagement or value-caused referral.

No E4+ event without a private evidence reference.

## Promotion threshold

Integrity Gate may move from **TEST** to **COMMERCIALLY VALIDATED PILOT** only when all are true:

1. at least 5 qualified buyer conversations;
2. at least 3 independently confirm the same repeated cross-surface reconciliation / change-propagation problem;
3. at least 2 say the €750 primary pilot is plausible, or propose an equivalent paid scope/budget path;
4. at least 1 reaches E7 activation;
5. the first activated run completes with genuine qualified human review and no unresolved control failure.

Do not call this product-market fit.

## Kill / pivot rule

After 10 qualified target-buyer conversations, if fewer than 2 buyers confirm repeated pain and no one reaches E6, stop expanding the product. Change buyer, problem, scope, packaging or channel before writing more software.

If buyers consistently value EUDAMED data preparation but not cross-surface integrity, reposition the engine as an internal delivery primitive rather than forcing a standalone product.

## Conversion sequence

`free tool / research / referral → quantified workload → qualified conversation → P1 price probe → written bounded scope → commercial activation → private intake → Integrity Gate → genuine human review → measured economics → delivery → payment / repeat decision`

The only metric that ultimately matters is whether this sequence repeatedly produces E7→E9 outcomes at acceptable delivery effort.
