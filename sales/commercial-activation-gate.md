# ClinicOps commercial activation gate

Status: **internal commercial control**. This is not a public price list, legal contract, invoice template or promise of regulatory outcome.

## Purpose

Convert genuine buying intent into controlled cash flow before ClinicOps commits material delivery capacity.

A buyer saying “sounds useful” is not activation. A pilot starts only when the agreed scope, commercial owner and payment/procurement path are explicit.

## Standard activation sequence

`qualified conversation → written scope → written acceptance → commercial activation condition → private evidence intake → delivery → acceptance / balance → expansion decision`

Before material client work begins, confirm privately:

1. the buyer and billing / procurement owner are identified;
2. the exact evidence population and deliverables are agreed in writing;
3. target timing has been agreed only after input quality is understood;
4. commercial terms are agreed in the private scope copy;
5. one of the approved activation conditions below is satisfied;
6. any real portfolio material will stay in approved private storage and out of the public repository.

## Approved activation conditions

Choose the condition appropriate to the buyer before work begins. Do not hard-code a universal percentage in public or reusable materials.

### A. Upfront payment

Best for a deliberately small first engagement when procurement friction is low.

Work begins after agreed payment is received.

### B. Deposit / first milestone

Best where the scope is larger or naturally milestone-based.

Work begins after the agreed first payment milestone is received. The remaining payment event must be defined before kickoff.

### C. Accepted PO / signed commercial commitment with invoice schedule

May be appropriate for an established organisation whose procurement process does not allow immediate card/bank prepayment.

Before kickoff, confirm the PO / signed commitment, invoicing entity, invoice recipient, payment terms and internal buyer owner. Do not treat an informal “go ahead” as equivalent to a procurement commitment.

### D. Explicit design-partner exception

A no-fee or reduced-fee design-partner pilot is an exception, not the default.

A design-partner scope is outside the standard delegated workflow. Do not activate one as a transaction-level exception. It may run only under a separately approved, time-bounded design-partner policy whose private scope states the non-cash learning value, for example:

- access to a real target workflow / bounded evidence set;
- structured feedback after delivery;
- a follow-up evaluation conversation;
- permission to retain strictly anonymised / aggregate product learning where appropriate;
- a credible path to a paid next scope if the pilot proves useful.

Do not provide unlimited free work in exchange for vague “exposure”. Keep design-partner work deliberately small and rare until the core paid offer is validated.

## Standard paid-pilot delegation envelope

The Class III Transition Map Pilot may run without founder transaction-level approval only when every condition below is satisfied:

1. the buyer is eligible under `EXP-001`;
2. the evidence population is deliberately bounded in the written scope;
3. deliverables, limitations and acceptance criteria remain those in `sales/first-pilot-scope-template.md`;
4. the commercial terms and activation condition use a pre-approved standard;
5. real portfolio evidence stays in approved private storage and no patient-identifiable data is requested or accepted;
6. the current controlled bundle schema and evidence boundaries remain unchanged;
7. a qualified, named human reviewer is assigned before kickoff and completes the final review gate; and
8. the scope creates no novel public claim, custom regulatory conclusion, non-standard liability or unsupported interpretation.

Within this envelope, a trained operator is authorised to move the pilot through written scope, commercial activation, private intake, controlled analysis, qualified human review, bundle delivery and closeout. Human regulatory review remains mandatory, but routine execution does not require Ali's approval.

### Automatic abstention boundary

Do not activate, improvise or silently widen a transaction when any envelope condition is unmet. Mark it **NON-STANDARD — NOT ACTIVATED** and record the reason privately.

The delegated workflow abstains from:

- no-fee or reduced-fee design-partner work;
- unusual legal, IP, indemnity, security, exclusivity or extended-payment terms;
- material discounts or custom pricing structures outside an approved standard;
- custom deliverables, unbounded populations or scope expansion;
- safety-critical disputes or unsupported regulatory interpretations;
- work where qualified independent human review cannot be secured; and
- any request involving patient-identifiable data or an unapproved storage route.

These are policy exceptions, not transaction-level judgement calls. They remain parked or declined until a separate policy-level decision changes the approved envelope. The operating workflow flags them; it does not route them to Ali for ad hoc approval.

### Delegation proof test

Treat founder-independent execution as proven only after the next eligible paid pilot, or two complete controlled dry runs, moves from qualified conversation through closeout with:

- zero Ali transaction-level decisions;
- every existing evidence, claim, commercial, data-handling and human-review gate satisfied;
- no unplanned scope, claim or commercial exception; and
- the commercial learning captured privately against `EXP-001`.

If either proof run needs an ad hoc founder decision or qualified review cannot be secured safely and economically, stop claiming founder-independent execution. Record the failed decision class, narrow the envelope and keep that class in automatic abstention.

## Cash-protection rules

- No open-ended delivery before an activation condition is met.
- Do not expand the agreed population silently; scope additions become a written change / next scope.
- Do not provide repeated unpaid analysis through email after a discovery conversation.
- If procurement delays activation, preserve the proposed scope but do not consume delivery capacity indefinitely.
- If a buyer needs a long vendor-onboarding process, decide whether the expected contract value and repeat potential justify it before investing heavily.
- Do not offer regulatory/compliance guarantees as a payment inducement.
- Payment is for the agreed professional service and deliverables, not for a guaranteed regulatory decision or authority outcome.

## Delivery-to-cash closeout

Before final release where the agreed commercial structure requires a remaining payment / milestone:

1. confirm the final human-review gate is complete;
2. confirm the client-facing bundle matches scope;
3. issue / confirm the applicable invoice or payment milestone;
4. follow the agreed delivery/payment sequence;
5. record privately whether payment arrived on time and whether procurement created friction;
6. ask the evidence-based expansion question only after the delivered work has demonstrated value.

Do not withhold safety-critical information in a way that creates harm. A safety-critical or commercial dispute is outside the standard envelope: stop routine release, preserve the evidence and obtain the appropriate independent professional handling. Do not improvise through the regulatory deliverable.

## Expansion question

After a successful delivery, identify the next real operational trigger rather than manufacturing urgency:

- another manufacturer / device family;
- a broader evidence population;
- a certificate / identifier / SS(C)P change event;
- repeat market-document QA;
- periodic portfolio integrity refresh;
- white-label AR / consultancy batch work.

If no repeat need exists, do not force a subscription.

## Cashflow evidence to capture privately

For each activated scope:

- scope value and currency;
- activation condition used;
- date scope sent;
- date accepted;
- date invoice / payment request issued;
- date activation condition satisfied;
- delivery effort hours;
- external costs;
- date final delivery accepted;
- date final payment received where applicable;
- procurement friction / payment delay;
- gross-effort signal;
- repeat / expansion / referral outcome.

Never put prospect/client-sensitive commercial details into the public repository.

## Decision rules

### Keep the commercial structure

Payment occurs with tolerable friction, delivery effort is economically sensible, and the buyer can understand the activation condition without damaging trust.

### Refine

Buyers want the service but the payment structure repeatedly blocks reasonable procurement. Change the private terms or milestone design before changing the underlying offer.

### Abstain / flag

Unusual legal terms, indemnities, guarantees, IP demands, security obligations, extended payment terms, exclusivity, major discounts or other material liabilities are outside the standard envelope. Mark the scope **NON-STANDARD — NOT ACTIVATED** and do not accept it through the delegated workflow.

### Stop

Do not continue investing delivery time where neither cash nor a deliberately approved design-partner learning return exists.

## Relationship to the pilot template

Use this gate together with `sales/first-pilot-scope-template.md`.

The pilot template defines **what** will be delivered. This file defines **when ClinicOps is commercially activated to begin delivering it**.
