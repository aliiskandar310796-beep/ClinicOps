## Corrections to preserve

### 1. Do not say the distributor must verify the notified-body number whenever one is legally required

The Court drew a narrower line.

A distributor is **not required to independently verify whether the device should be classified in risk class IIa**. Where the information available to the distributor shows that the **manufacturer has classified the device in a class that necessarily involves a notified body**, the distributor's due-care obligation includes checking that the four-digit identification number of that notified body is indicated.

This is a consistency check against information available to the distributor, not a second classification/conformity assessment.

Relevant judgment paragraphs: 52-66; operative part point 2.

### 2. Treat the instructions for use as an input to the consistency check, not as the object of the Court's operative ruling

Paragraph 38 identifies the EU declaration of conformity, CE marking and instructions for use among the documents enabling the distributor to carry out the necessary consistency checks. The Court also notes that the manufacturer's website and promotional material may provide relevant information.

The operative answer to questions 1 and 2 is framed around whether, in light of the information at the distributor's disposal, the **CE marking and EU declaration of conformity clearly concern a device within the MDR**.

Relevant judgment paragraphs: 37-38 and 51; operative part point 1.

## Corrected LinkedIn draft

### MDR distributor checks after C-10/24: consistency, not a second conformity assessment

The Court of Justice has now clarified an important boundary in Article 14 MDR.

A distributor must act with due care and, using the information available to it, check whether the CE marking and EU declaration of conformity clearly concern a device falling under the MDR.

That can require looking across the documents and information already available to the distributor. The Court specifically mentions the declaration of conformity, CE marking and instructions for use as useful inputs, and notes that a manufacturer's website or promotional material can also matter.

But the distributor is **not required to independently re-classify the device**.

For the notified-body number, the distinction is important: if the information available shows that the manufacturer has classified the device in a risk class that necessarily involves a notified body, due care includes checking that the four-digit notified-body identification number is indicated.

So the practical workflow is not:

**"redo the manufacturer's conformity assessment."**

It is closer to:

**"check the available evidence for clear inconsistencies, and escalate when the information gives reason to doubt conformity."**

The judgment also gives useful guidance on escalation: a competitor's formal notice can create reason to believe a product is non-conforming; a distributor may rely on the manufacturer's reasoned response unless it appears manifestly unfounded; and a reasoned, clear opinion from the competent authority can dispel the doubts described in Article 14(2).

Case C-10/24, Dürr Dental, Court of Justice, 4 June 2026, ECLI:EU:C:2026:443.

## Short version

C-10/24 does **not** turn distributors into substitute notified bodies.

The Court says Article 14 MDR requires a due-care **consistency check** using the information available to the distributor. The distributor does not have to independently determine whether a device belongs in class IIa.

If the manufacturer's own classification indicates a class that necessarily involves a notified body, however, the distributor should check that the four-digit notified-body number is indicated.

And the instructions for use are best understood here as one source of information for the consistency check — not the object of the operative ruling.
