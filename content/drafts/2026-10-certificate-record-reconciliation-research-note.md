# A certificate record that can answer a query

## Why this note

A manufacturer's certificate exists in more than one place: the document the notified body issued, the certificate record in EUDAMED, and the SS(C)P linkage pointing at the same device. When a notified body, an authorised representative, an importer or an authority asks "is this certificate yours, and does it cover this device?", the answer is assembled from fields each source holds separately. The practical question is whether the manufacturer answers in hours from a reconciled row, or only after an investigation.

## What the registry supports

- The manufacturer draws up the SS(C)P for implantable and class III devices, subject to the Article 32 exclusions, and Article 32(2)(a) requires it to identify the device, including the Basic UDI-DI. The Basic UDI-DI is therefore printed in the document and comparable with the registration [1][2].
- MDCG 2026-4 recommends that, for devices placed on the market before mandatory use of the UDI/Devices module, manufacturers upload SS(C)Ps no later than 27 February 2027, with manufacturers and notified bodies aligning their timelines. It is a recommendation in a non-binding paper, not a statutory deadline. Dates on the manufacturer's side and the notified body's side are planned together [3].
- Under the SS(C)P document-control model MDCG 2019-9 rev.1 describes, an updated revision can exist while validation timing is in process, so a document-versus-record difference is not an irregularity until the process state is established [4].
- In the September 2026 public-source scan, in 22 consecutive rows no published SS(C)P and EUDAMED record carried the same reference number and revision, and the printed Basic UDI-DI matched no EUDAMED Basic UDI-DI in 6 of the 22. Convenience sample; aggregates only; identifiers, never text [5].

## The structure

One row per certificate, carrying the fields the sources repeat: the certificate holder's name and address as each source states it; the Basic UDI-DI(s) covered; every date each source carries (certificate issue and expiry, SS(C)P issue date, registration date), each with its source named; the scope line; the notified body's identity. For each field, one of four outcome states: match; difference, routed to a qualified reviewer; not comparable, because one source does not carry the field; unresolved, because the evidence cannot decide.

## The failure mode

A discrepancy with no outcome state attached reads, to an outside party, as a signal. The first explanation to test is ordinary document control: a name updated in one system only, a date transcribed differently, a device added to one record only. A reconciled row shows which explanation holds; an unreconciled one leaves a third party to guess, on its own timetable.

## Boundaries

Nothing here states what any notified body does with a discrepancy. What the notified-body association has published on that subject is written below as a candidate claim, not asserted. The list of fields a certificate must contain is likewise a candidate, not a registry claim. Nothing names a manufacturer, device or notified body; nothing is a compliance statement about any party.
