## Four fleets per segment

Every segment runs the same four bounded work cells.

### 1. Delivery fleet

Owns the sellable evidence outcome now:
- approved source / declared scope;
- observations and evidence references;
- deterministic comparison where appropriate;
- unresolved queue;
- accountable decision owner;
- reviewer-ready closure packet.

Delivery is also the primary source of product evidence. Privately capture the trigger, current workaround, common inputs, common outputs, manual/review steps, recurring failure modes, rework, turnaround, acceptance state, willingness-to-pay signal and repeat cadence where observable.

### 2. Distribution fleet

Maintains at least three acquisition routes per segment:
- inbound tools / scope brief;
- partner and referral development;
- research/content distribution;
- warm follow-up;
- bounded cold outreach only when global controls permit;
- events/professional communities where relevant.

No segment may rely on one channel for more than half of planned activity units when alternatives exist.

### 3. Recurring-revenue fleet

Looks for cadence created by real operations:
- protocol amendments and study changes;
- QMS/SOP/CAPA/supplier changes;
- AI vendor/model/version changes;
- lab method/equipment/competency changes;
- clinic vendor renewals and SOP/training changes;
- controlled medical-content/version changes;
- device regulatory-data/document lifecycle changes.

A subscription or maintenance retainer is activated only after cadence is confirmed.

### 4. Productization fleet

Converts only repeated delivery structures into lower-touch assets:
- browser-local checks;
- packet builders;
- evidence-register formats;
- deterministic change mappers;
- partner licenses;
- monitoring utilities.

The productization fleet automates evidence handling, not reserved clinical, regulatory, legal, safety, quality or release decisions.

It must not promote an idea into a core MVP because it appears broadly applicable. A core product candidate requires the hard paid-evidence gate in `PRODUCT_EXTRACTION_DOCTRINE_2026-09-16.md`.

## Weekly rebalancing algorithm

Each live segment is reviewed weekly against three inputs:

1. **Commercial evidence velocity — 60%**
   - qualified human responses;
   - buyer conversations;
   - repeated-pain confirmation;
   - scope/quote/procurement movement;
   - activation/payment/acceptance/repeat evidence.
2. **Conversion progress — 25%**
   - movement from awareness to scoped work;
   - partner-introduction quality;
   - conversion from free tool or content to real workflow discussion.
3. **Portfolio resilience — 15%**
   - dependence on one regulation, buyer type, geography, channel or tool;
   - delivery/expertise risk;
   - reusable evidence primitives across segments.

A single weekly rebalance may move at most five percentage points. Floors and ceilings remain binding unless a documented kill-switch condition applies.

## Kill switches

Pause or sharply reduce a segment when:
- the work requires expertise or authorization ClinicOps cannot responsibly provide;
- a privacy/integrity risk cannot be bounded;
- buyer conversations repeatedly contradict the core pain thesis;
- acquisition requires unsupported claims;
- delivery becomes bespoke with little reusable ClinicOps structure;
- a segment reaches the defined no-signal threshold without commercial progression.

## Company-wide distribution safety

All agents and segments share one outbound budget. Before any cold send, the company state must establish:
- current daily cold-email count;
- suppression status across CRM/mail history/permanent lists;
- domicile/jurisdiction restriction status;
- branded sender identity;
- duplicate/contact collision state.

The company cold-email ceiling is 15/day and Denmark-domiciled organizations are excluded from cold email. If the shared ledger cannot establish remaining capacity, cold sending fails closed while non-cold distribution continues.

## Operating metrics

Do not collapse company health into one score. Track separately:
- **commercial:** E-stage movement, activated work, payment, repeat/referral;
- **delivery:** accepted packets, closure quality, turnaround, rework;
- **distribution:** qualified reach by segment/channel, partner introductions, inbound briefs;
- **recurring:** workflows with confirmed cadence, retention/renewal;
- **technical:** CI, deterministic checks, portable fallback;
- **governance:** human review, claim boundaries, privacy containment, suppression compliance;
- **product extraction:** repeated paid cases, independent buyers/workflows, common I/O share, repeat purchases, measurable value and documented competitor gaps.

## Revenue architecture

The portfolio continuously supports three income modes:

1. **Active:** bounded reviews, remediation/change support, partner delivery.
2. **Recurring:** monitoring, register maintenance, evidence-health reviews, retainers.
3. **Lower-touch:** tools, templates, packet builders, licenses and research products only after repeated delivery proves the stable structure.

The company is therefore always running at three horizons: deliver now, convert recurring work, and extract reusable product primitives without prematurely turning hypotheses into software.

The preferred sequence is **service proof → standardized service → internal tooling → repeated buyer pull → core MVP candidate → software expansion**.
