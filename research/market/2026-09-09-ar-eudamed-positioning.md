# AR / EUDAMED market-positioning scan — 9 September 2026

Status: internal commercial research. Checked 9 September 2026. This is a positioning scan, not a legal assessment of competitors.

## Question

Where should ClinicOps compete or partner when established authorised representatives already sell EUDAMED registration and verification services?

## Current public positioning observed

### Obelis

Obelis publicly markets EUDAMED actor registration, SRN support, device registration, mandate management for non-EU manufacturers, and document preparation/review. Its broader EU authorised-representative offer includes EUDAMED registration and technical-document support.

Sources:
- https://www.obelis.net/medical/eu/eudamed/
- https://www.obelis.net/medical/eu/authorised-representative/

### MDSS

MDSS positions EUDAMED as mandatory and integrates EUDAMED verification into authorised-representative onboarding/submission work. Its public messaging includes UDI/data-structuring strategies, device-registration readiness and continuing EUDAMED registration support.

Sources:
- https://mdss.com/
- https://mdss.com/eudamed-milestone-reached-what-it-means-for-manufacturers-and-their-processes-with-mdss/
- https://mdss.com/goodbye-dmids-hello-eudamed/

### Emergo by UL

Emergo's public positioning is broader in-country representation and regulatory-market-access support across multiple jurisdictions, with large-scale authorised-representative coverage rather than a narrow EUDAMED transition analytics offer.

Sources:
- https://www.emergobyul.com/services/local-authorized-representative-medical-device-manufacturers
- https://www.emergobyul.com/services/uk-responsible-person-ukrp-medical-devices

## White-space conclusion

ClinicOps should **not** lead with "we can register your device in EUDAMED" or try to out-market established ARs on legal-representation breadth.

The more differentiated job is:

> turn supplied/public portfolio evidence into a role-aware, evidence-backed transition work queue that an AR, manufacturer or consultancy can use to decide what needs human attention first.

This makes ClinicOps potentially complementary to an AR rather than a substitute for one.

## Product implication

The Class III Transition Map should emphasise:

1. portfolio-level segmentation rather than single-device registration;
2. evidence reconciliation across public EUDAMED signals, certificate timing and manufacturer-controlled records;
3. explicit missing-evidence queues;
4. operator prioritisation rather than compliance scoring;
5. repeatability across multiple manufacturer portfolios;
6. machine-readable output that can feed an AR's own workflow;
7. white-label / partner use where commercially appropriate.

## Conversation implication for Obelis and similar ARs

Do not pitch basic EUDAMED registration capability. Ask instead:

- Where does portfolio reconciliation still consume manual time after registration services are already in place?
- Can the AR see transition timing and evidence gaps across multiple manufacturer portfolios in one repeatable queue?
- Which evidence joins are repeatedly rebuilt by staff?
- Would a sanitized/machine-readable work-plan layer reduce internal preparation time without changing the AR's legal role or client relationship?

## Kill criteria

If established ARs consistently say their existing systems already provide this portfolio-level prioritisation with negligible manual reconciliation, do not build a competing dashboard. Shift the engine toward manufacturers/regulatory consultancies or another evidence-heavy workflow.

## Strategic posture

**Partner-first where possible.** ClinicOps' moat should be evidence discipline + reproducible portfolio operations + human-reviewed prioritisation, not possession of the client mandate or a generic registration form.
