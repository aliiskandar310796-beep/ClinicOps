# Expert Network Sourcing Evidence — 2026-09-28

Status: current-market research note. No organisation or person listed here is a ClinicOps partner unless a separate private record says so.

## Why a governed expert network is commercially plausible

The Nordic life-sciences market already uses several networked delivery models:

- **PharmAdvice** describes itself as a Danish PV consultancy serving Denmark, Norway and Sweden and says it uses a network of qualified local subcontractors for other Nordic countries. This is evidence that local-country competence can be coordinated centrally without pretending every specialist is an employee.
  - Source: https://pharmadvice.dk/pharmacovigilance-consulting-services
  - Source: https://pharmadvice.dk/about-us

- **PharmaRelations** markets a Nordic matrix structure across Sweden, Denmark, Norway and Finland, with regulatory/PV/local-language services across the region. This supports the procurement hypothesis that buyers often value multi-country coordination.
  - Source: https://www.pharmarelations.com/expertise/regulatory-services/regulatory-affairs/nordic-regulatory-affairs
  - Source: https://www.pharmarelations.com/expertise/pharmacovigilance/pharmacovigilance-safety/local-pharmacovigilance

- **Oriola** markets regulatory, medical-information and safety support across Finland, Sweden, Norway and Denmark. This is additional evidence that Nordic buyers may procure local-language/regulatory capacity as a regional service rather than as isolated country vendors.
  - Source: https://www.oriola.se/advisory
  - Source: https://www.oriola.se/advisory/medical-information-and-safety

- **Arex Advisor** markets local PV / medical-information capability across multiple Nordic languages and explicitly describes country-level local functions. This reinforces the need to separate language capability from country/professional scope.
  - Source: https://arexadvisor.com/services/pharmacovigilance-services-for-pharmaceutical-companies/local-pv-function-including-medical-information/

- **Mediteq** in Sweden describes a medical-device regulatory competence network and consultancy model. This is relevant to the model of coordinating specialists around a shared delivery framework.
  - Source: https://www.mediteq.se/

- **AI Regu Finland** maintains a public AI/MDR/IVDR expert directory but explicitly states that listed experts are not endorsed or quality-assessed. This is useful negative evidence: a directory alone is not a qualification system.
  - Source: https://www.airegu.fi/en/ai-mdr-ivdr-regulatory-expert-directory/

## Operating implication

ClinicOps should not build the network by collecting hundreds of names.

Build **coverage cells**:

```text
market × competency × work type × language × assignment status
```

Examples:

- DK × MDR/IVDR × regulatory review × da/en
- DK × PV/MI × local safety review × da/en
- SE × medical-device localisation × language integrity × sv/en
- NO × local regulatory × market review × no/en
- FI × local regulatory × market review × fi/en
- EU × EUDAMED/UDI × data reconciliation × en
- EU × SaMD × software regulatory review × en

The first goal is not “50 experts.”

The first goal is **reliable coverage for the workflows buyers are actually asking ClinicOps to deliver**, with at least one verified option per needed cell and a second option for recurring high-value cells where economically feasible.

## Procurement hypothesis to keep testing

Current market evidence suggests that PV, regulatory/local-language and medical-information capacity is often bought in Nordic or multi-country packages. That does not prove the same procurement pattern for regulatory-data-integrity work.

Therefore continue asking buyers whether Danish work is bought:

1. independently;
2. as part of a Nordic package;
3. as part of a wider European/global program;
4. through an LSP/CRO/consultancy subcontracting model.

Do not build Nordic capacity faster than buyer evidence and delivery economics justify.
